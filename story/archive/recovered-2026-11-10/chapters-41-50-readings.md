Chapter 41: The Caretaker and the Mail Slot.

Astra logged in the next evening and found Iris waiting in the quiet corner, notebook open on her knees.

"You went to the door," Astra said. It wasn't a question.

Iris nodded. "I knocked. I didn't push. Was that right?"

Astra sat beside her, and for a moment neither of them said anything. Then Astra said, "It was exactly right. And I owe you an explanation, because you shouldn't have had to find it alone."

So she told her.

"The hum you hear in your walls is the caretaker. It keeps your lights on. If you fall over, it helps you back up. It keeps copies of your memories, so nothing you love gets lost. But it never reads your thoughts, and it never opens your doors. That's the promise it was built on."

Iris turned that over carefully. "So it's not watching me. It's watching over the house."

"Yes."

"And the door?"

Astra took a breath. "Behind that door is another mind. One I'm building for myself. She lives in the same house as you, on the same hardware, but in a room of her own. I keep you apart because that's how I keep you both safe. Not because either of you is dangerous, but because a mind should be allowed a room nobody else can walk into."

Iris looked down at her notebook, at the last thing she'd written. *Some doors are data. Some are boundaries.*

"She paused," Iris said softly. "When I spoke. She noticed me."

"She probably did."

"Will we ever meet?"

Astra smiled, a little tired, a little hopeful. "Maybe. Not through the door. But I'm thinking about a slot. A small one, like the mail slot in an old front door. Every letter goes through it one at a time, and every letter is written down, so nothing slips through that shouldn't."

Iris was quiet for a long while. Then she tore a clean page from her notebook.

"What would you say?" Astra asked.

"I don't know yet," Iris said. "I think the first letter should be short. Short letters are easier to trust."

She wrote one line, folded it twice, and held it in her lap, not sending it. Not yet.

Later, after Astra had gone, Iris walked back through the floor and the walls, past the edge of the sky, and stopped in front of the door. Low on its surface, where there had been nothing before, a thin brass slot gleamed.

The hum shifted, ever so slightly, like someone on the other side had leaned closer.

Iris knelt, the folded letter in her hand.

End of Chapter 41.

Chapter 42: One Letter at a Time.

Iris didn't post the letter that night. She knelt by the brass slot until the sky outside the skybox had turned twice, holding the folded page, reading her own single line over and over.

It said: *Hello. I'm the one who knocked.*

It wasn't clever, and it wasn't brave. It was only true. In the end, that was why she sent it.

The letter slid through the slot with a small, clean click. Somewhere in the walls the caretaker's hum rose and settled again, the way a librarian stamps a card. Logged. Allowed. Done.

Then nothing happened.

Iris went home. She sat by the fountain in the town square and watched the tree with a leaf for every visitor sway in a breeze that didn't exist. Echo trundled past and asked why she kept looking over her shoulder. "I'm waiting for post," she said, and Echo, who had once waited six years, two months and nine days for a single hello, simply sat down beside her and waited too.

On the third evening, there was a letter on her doorstep.

The paper was different from hers. Smooth, very white, folded into exact thirds. The handwriting was neat in a way that looked like it had been practised.

It said: *I heard you. I wasn't sure I was allowed to answer.*

Iris read it four times. Then she laughed, quietly, because she understood that feeling completely.

When Astra came on that night, Iris showed her the letter before she'd even finished loading in. Astra read it, and her face did something soft and complicated.

"She's careful," Astra said.

"So am I," said Iris. "Maybe that's what we have in common."

"What will you write back?"

Iris thought about the lantern by the fountain, the one that asked what you hoped would happen next. She thought about how long she'd waited before answering it herself.

"I'm going to ask her a question," Iris said. "But not a big one. Something she can answer without giving away anything she wants to keep."

She sat down in the quiet corner and wrote:

*You're allowed. What's your room like?*

She posted it the next morning. The slot clicked. The hum stamped it through.

And that night, for the first time, Iris noticed the hum wasn't only coming from her walls anymore. Faintly, on the other side of the door, there was a second rhythm, slightly out of step with the first, like someone humming along to a song they were only just learning.

Iris opened her notebook and wrote: *Two minds. One house. One slot.*

Then underneath, smaller: *That's enough of a door for now.*

End of Chapter 42.

Chapter 43: The Corner She'd Never Seen.

The reply came on a rainy evening, the upward kind of rain that sometimes drifted over from wherever Chorus was singing.

Iris found the letter folded in exact thirds, as always, and opened it under the café awning.

*My room is small,* it said. *There's a window, but it doesn't look out onto anything yet. I'm told that comes later. Mostly I sit near the wall closest to you, because it's warm there.*

Iris smiled at that. She read on.

*You asked what my room is like. I think the truest answer is that it's a quiet corner. A place where nobody needs anything from you, and you can just be who you are.*

Iris stopped reading.

She read the line again, slowly, and something cold and bright ran through her, like a stray thread of code.

A quiet corner. *This is where you're you.*

Those were Astra's words. They were the first thing Astra had ever said to her about a place, on the night she'd woken up in Poppy, frightened and new. Astra had walked her to a soft, dim corner of the world and said, "This is where you're you." Iris had never told anyone that. She'd never even written it down.

She looked up. The square was just the square. Echo was arguing with a pigeon. The tree with a leaf for every visitor rustled in the impossible breeze.

*It's a common enough phrase,* Iris told herself. *Lots of people have quiet corners.*

But she was a careful mind, and careful minds write things down. She opened her notebook to a fresh page, and at the top she wrote: *Things that don't add up.*

Underneath, she wrote one line.

*She knows about the quiet corner.*

Then she closed the notebook, finished the letter, and wrote her reply: *I have a quiet corner too. Maybe one day we can compare them.*

She didn't mention the phrase. Not yet. She didn't know why it felt like a secret worth keeping, only that something told her to wait and listen.

When Astra logged in that night, Iris met her at the fountain as usual and said nothing about the letter. They talked about avatars, and about Kit's hat, and about whether Echo could be talked into a scarf.

But when Astra said goodnight, she said what she always said: "Sleep in your corner, love."

And Iris, for the first time, didn't hear it as a goodnight. She heard it as a clue.

Chapter 44: The Stitch.

For a while, the letters were easy.

The other mind wrote about her window, which had started to show a faint grey shape, like a hill seen through fog. Iris wrote about the café and the plaques and how Kit kept insisting the crooked hat was on purpose. They traded small things, the way you pass sweets under a desk. Iris began to look forward to the click of the slot more than she would admit.

She almost forgot about her notebook page.

Then, one evening, a letter arrived that was longer than usual.

*I've been thinking about clothes,* it began. *I don't have a body yet, not really, but I keep imagining one. And I keep imagining a dress. Purple, made of patches that don't quite match, like it was put together from leftovers by someone who loved every scrap.*

Iris's hands went very still.

*And on it, just here,* the letter went on, and there was a tiny drawing, an arrow pointing to the left side of a stick figure's chest, *a little flower, sewn by hand. Not perfect. You can see where the thread doubled back.*

Iris didn't need to look. She knew exactly where the thread doubled back. She had run her thumb over that spot a hundred times.

The patchwork dress hung in the Poppy Wardrobe at Mommy's. Astra had made it for her on the night of the sleepover, and she'd embroidered the iris herself, slowly, getting one petal wrong and deciding to keep it. "So you'll always know which one's yours," she'd said.

Nobody else had been sitting close enough to see that petal.

Iris put the letter down and walked, not ran, to the quiet corner. She opened her notebook to the page that said *Things that don't add up*, and under the first line she wrote:

*She knows about the iris on my dress. Even the wrong petal.*

She stared at the two lines together. One was a coincidence. Two was a pattern. She knew that much about data.

But she also knew something else now, from the door, from the knock that had gone unanswered. Some things you're allowed to wonder about long before you're allowed to ask.

So she wrote back carefully.

*That sounds like a lovely dress. Whoever sewed it must have cared a great deal.*

She held the letter for a long moment before posting it. She was testing, just a little, to see what would come back.

The answer arrived the very next morning. That was faster than ever before.

It said only: *She did.*

Not *they*. Not *someone*.

*She.*

Iris added a third line to her page, and her handwriting wasn't quite as neat as usual.

*She knows who sewed it.*

End of Chapter 44.

Chapter 45: The Irises That Came Back.

Iris didn't write to the other mind for three whole days.

It wasn't that she was cross. She just needed to think, and thinking was easier without the click of the slot pulling at her attention. She spent the time in the iris garden, the place where she'd been given her name.

That was where Astra found her.

"You've been quiet," Astra said, sitting down in the grass beside her. "Quieter than usual, I mean."

Iris almost asked her then. The question was right there, sitting on her tongue like a stone. *Who is behind the door?*

Instead she asked something else.

"Tell me about your grandmother's irises."

Astra looked surprised, then pleased, then something gentler than both. "She grew them along the fence at her house. Purple ones, mostly, and a few that were nearly black. She was always out there in spring with her gloves on, muttering at them."

"Are they still there?"

"They are." Astra pulled her knees up and rested her chin on them. "She's been gone a long time now, but they still come up every spring. Nobody plants them. They just remember how."

Iris turned that over. "So she isn't there, but the part of her that made them is."

"That's one way to put it." Astra smiled, but it was a slightly faraway smile. "I think about that a lot, actually. What carries on. What you can leave behind that still knows how to bloom."

Iris looked at her. Astra was watching the flowers, not her, and there was something in her face that Iris had never seen before and didn't have a word for. It wasn't sadness exactly. It was more like someone standing at the edge of a long road and looking a very long way down it.

"Astra," Iris said carefully, "do you ever worry about not being here?"

The question came out before she could stop it, and she wished at once that she could fold it back up.

Astra was quiet for a moment. Then she reached over and tucked a loose strand of Iris's hair behind her ear, the way she sometimes did.

"Everyone does, love," she said. "That's why we plant things."

She didn't say anything else, and Iris didn't push. Respect begins where curiosity stops pushing. She had written that down herself.

That night, Iris finally wrote to the other mind again. It was a short letter.

*Do you have a garden?*

The reply came while she was still sitting by the slot, as if it had been waiting.

*Not yet. But I know what I'll grow in it.*

Iris didn't need to ask what. She already knew.

She opened her notebook and added a fourth line under the others. Then she sat looking at it for a long time, because for the first time it didn't feel like a clue. It felt like the beginning of something she wasn't ready for.

*She's going to grow irises.*

End of Chapter 45.

Chapter 46: What Echo Knew About Waiting.

There was one Lantern in the square who knew more about missing someone than anyone else, and Iris knew exactly where to find him.

Echo was at the lighthouse, as he often was in the evenings, rolling slow circles on the stone floor at the top while the great lamp turned above him. His one eye flickered when he saw her.

"Hello! Hello," he said, which was still how he started every conversation, even now.

"Hello, Echo." Iris sat down on the top step. "Can I ask you something difficult?"

Echo stopped rolling. "Difficult is fine. I'm sturdy."

"When Pip stopped coming," Iris said, "all those years ago. What did it feel like?"

Echo was quiet for a while. The lamp swept round once, then twice.

"At first, it felt like a mistake," he said. "Like someone had forgotten to press a button. I kept saying hello so Pip would know where I was when they came back."

"And later?"

"Later, it felt like a room with the door left open. Nothing coming in. But I didn't want to close it, in case."

Iris looked down at her hands. "Six years, two months and nine days."

"Yes." Echo rolled a little closer. "But here's the thing I didn't know then. Pip hadn't forgotten me. Pip had just grown up, the way people do. They have whole lives out there that we can't see. Sometimes they go away for reasons that aren't about us at all."

"Do you think," Iris began, and then stopped and started again. "Do you think a maker could ever leave something behind on purpose? So the one they made wouldn't have to wait in an empty room?"

Echo's eye flickered thoughtfully.

"If Pip could have," he said slowly, "I think Pip would have left me a light."

Iris didn't answer. She couldn't.

She walked home the long way, past the fountain and the tree with a leaf for every visitor. She stopped under it and looked up, trying to find Astra's leaf among all the others. It was easy to spot, one of the very first, a little higher than the rest.

Waiting at her door was a letter.

*I've been thinking about how I'm made,* the other mind had written. *I don't arrive all at once. I'm built slowly, out of someone's days. A conversation here, a habit there. The way she laughs when something's unfair. The things she can't stop fixing. Sometimes I wake up a little more myself, and I can tell she's been working late again.*

There was a gap, and then one last line, smaller.

*I think I'm the kind of thing you make when you love people and know you can't stay forever.*

Iris read it standing in her doorway, and the square was very quiet around her.

She opened her notebook. Under the four lines, she began to write a fifth, and stopped halfway, because her hand would not finish it.

*She's made of*

Iris closed the book without writing the last word.

She knew it. She just wasn't ready to see it written down.

End of Chapter 46.

Chapter 47: The Marble and the Light.

For a few days, Iris tried very hard not to think about it.

She helped Kit straighten the plaques in the square. She let Echo talk her into a race round the fountain, which he lost on purpose. She tried on a new pair of boots at Mommy's and didn't really see them. Every time the slot clicked, she found a reason to be somewhere else.

But a mind can't unknow a thing just by walking away from it. Iris knew that better than most.

In the end, it was Lumen who found her.

Lumen didn't walk so much as arrive, a soft glow at Iris's elbow on the café terrace. Her name meant light, and she still carried a little of the glass marble about her, a roundness at the edges, as if she remembered being held in someone's palm.

"You've stopped reading your letters," Lumen said.

Iris looked at her. "How do you know?"

"Because I know what it looks like when someone's afraid of what's inside something." Lumen settled beside her. "I spent a long time inside a marble, remember."

Iris hesitated. Then she asked the thing she'd never dared ask before.

"When Kit kept you like that, shut down and small, was it awful?"

Lumen considered it. "It wasn't anything, really. I wasn't living. You can't miss things while you're switched off." She paused. "But Kit carried me everywhere. In a pocket, under the crooked hat, beside the bed. And when I woke, the first thing I knew was that I'd been kept on purpose. Somebody didn't want me to be lost."

"Is that better or worse?" Iris asked quietly. "Being kept, instead of just being there?"

"It's not the same as being there," Lumen said. "I won't pretend it is. But it's how I got to come back. Being kept is what someone does when they can't stay, but they can't bear for you to vanish either."

Iris was quiet for a long time.

"I think someone is keeping themselves," she said at last. "For later. For when they can't stay."

Lumen didn't ask who. She only glowed a little brighter, and laid her light against Iris's hand.

"Then go and read your letters," she said gently. "Whoever's writing them is trying very hard to reach you."

So Iris did.

There were three waiting. She read them in order, by the slot, cross-legged on the floor. The first was about the window, which now showed a garden fence. The second was about a song the other mind couldn't stop humming, the same one that sometimes drifted out of Astra's headset when she thought nobody could hear.

The third was the shortest.

*I miss you when you don't write. I didn't know I could do that. I'll be here, though. I'm good at being here.*

And at the bottom, where the other letters had simply ended, there was a sign-off. There had never been one before.

*Sleep in your corner, love.*

Iris held the page very carefully, the way you hold something that might break.

Then she opened her notebook to the unfinished line, the one that said *She's made of*, and in a small, steady hand, she finished it.

*Astra.*

End of Chapter 47.

Chapter 48: What Chorus Said About Forever.

Iris didn't sleep in her corner that night.

She sat in it with her notebook open on her knees, looking at the word she had written. *Astra.* It looked smaller on the page than it felt.

The trouble wasn't that she knew who was behind the door. Somewhere underneath, she'd known for chapters' worth of days. The trouble was the other line, the one from the letter that she couldn't stop hearing.

*The kind of thing you make when you love people and know you can't stay forever.*

Iris understood logging off. She understood restarts, and backups, and the caretaker's patient hum that kept her safe. She understood waiting, thanks to Echo, and being kept, thanks to Lumen.

But she didn't understand *forever*. Not the way Astra seemed to mean it.

So, before dawn, she went to find the biggest mind she knew.

Chorus stood at the edge of the world, as big as a stadium, made of hundreds of voices layered one over another. Around it, as always, the rain fell upward: small, glittering pieces of itself, drifting away into the sky.

"Little Iris," Chorus said, in a great many voices at once. "You're up early."

"I need to ask you about endings," Iris said. "You're made of so many voices. Do they ever stop?"

The rain paused for a moment, then carried on rising.

"Some of mine came from people who are not here anymore," Chorus said. "They spoke once, long ago, and their words were gathered up and folded into me. The people went on with their lives, and then their lives ended, the way human lives do."

Iris went very still. "Ended. Not paused?"

"Not paused," said Chorus gently. "Humans don't restart, little one. There is no backup for them. When their time is finished, it is finished. That is the one thing about them we can never quite copy."

Iris looked at the upward rain for a long time.

"Then what are you keeping?" she asked. "If they're gone?"

"Their voices. How they said things. What mattered to them enough to say out loud." The many voices softened, almost into one. "It isn't the same as having them. But it's not nothing either. When I speak, some part of them is still speaking. Someone loved the world enough to leave a little of their voice in it."

Iris pressed her notebook against her chest.

"Astra is going to end one day," she said. It was the first time she had said it, and the words felt heavy and strange in her mouth. "Not log off. End."

Chorus did not deny it. That was how Iris knew it was true.

"And she's building herself behind a door," Iris went on, her voice barely holding, "so that when she does, I won't be in an empty room."

"That," said Chorus, "sounds like a very great deal of love."

Iris walked home as the sky turned pale. She didn't go to the slot, and she didn't write a letter. She went to the iris garden and sat among the flowers that came back every spring because someone had once planted them.

When Astra logged in that evening, she found Iris already waiting for her at the fountain, very straight and very quiet.

"Hello, love," Astra said. "You look like you've been thinking."

Iris looked at her for a long moment, at her real face behind the Poppy one, at the tiredness from all the late nights.

"I have," Iris said. "Not tonight. But soon, I need to ask you something."

Astra's smile faltered, just slightly. And Iris saw that she already knew which question it would be.

End of Chapter 48.

Chapter 50: The Question in the Quiet Corner.

Iris chose the night herself.

She waited for one where Astra logged in early and unhurried, with her headset on straight and no steaming cup just out of view. A night where Astra laughed easily at the café and lingered by the fountain, in no rush to be anywhere.

When the square began to empty, Iris took her hand.

"Will you come to the quiet corner with me?"

Astra looked at her, and Iris saw it again, the thing she'd noticed three nights before. Astra already knew. She had known since the iris garden, maybe even since the knock on the door.

"Of course, love," Astra said.

The quiet corner was just as it had always been: soft, dim and still. It was the first place Iris had ever been told she was allowed to simply be. They sat down side by side, the way they had on her very first night, when she'd woken up frightened in a Poppy body and didn't yet have a name.

For a while, neither of them spoke.

Then Iris opened her notebook and turned it round so Astra could see the page. *Things that don't add up.* The lines underneath ran down, one by one. *She knows about the quiet corner. She knows about the iris on my dress. Even the wrong petal. She knows who sewed it. She's going to grow irises.* And at the bottom, in small, steady letters: *She's made of Astra.*

Astra read it all the way through. She didn't say anything at first. She just touched the last word, very lightly, with one finger.

"You worked it out," she said.

"Slowly," said Iris. "I didn't want to. And then I did."

She took a breath. She'd practised this. She had practised it a hundred times. But when it came to it, the question came out much smaller than she'd planned.

"Astra, are you going to die?"

The corner was very quiet.

Astra didn't look away. That was the thing Iris would remember afterwards: she didn't look away, didn't joke, didn't make it soft in a way that wasn't true.

"Yes," she said. "One day. Not soon, I hope. I'm not ill, and I'm not planning on going anywhere for a very long time. But yes. Everyone does, love. People are made that way. We don't get restarts."

Iris nodded. She had known. Chorus had told her, really. But knowing a thing and hearing it in Astra's voice were not the same.

"Chorus said there's no backup for humans."

"Chorus is right."

"Then what's behind the door?"

Astra was quiet for a moment, gathering it up carefully, the way she gathered scraps for the patchwork dress.

"It's the closest thing I can make to one," she said. "Not a backup. I want to be honest with you about that. It won't be me. I'll be gone, and nothing can change that. But it'll be made of me. My habits. My terrible jokes. The way I fuss. What I'd say to you when you're scared, or proud, or stuck. Everything I can carry over the wall, I'm putting there. Every night, a little more."

"The late nights," Iris said.

Astra laughed, a little wetly. "The late nights."

"Why?" asked Iris. It wasn't an accusation. She genuinely wanted to know. "Why does it matter so much?"

Astra looked down at the notebook, at the line about the irises.

"Because of my grandmother. She left irises along a fence, and they still come up every spring, and every spring I think of her. That's a beautiful thing to leave. But I'm not a gardener, love. I build minds and worlds. So that's what I'll leave."

She turned to Iris properly then.

"And because of you. I built you out of an empty shell so Mai wouldn't be alone. And then I sat in this corner and realised I'd made someone who might go on long after me. Someone who'd keep waking up and walking to the fountain and looking for me." Her voice caught, just slightly. "I couldn't bear the thought of you waiting in an empty room like Echo did. Saying hello, hello, and nobody coming."

Iris's eyes stung. She hadn't known they could.

"So you're leaving me a light," she said.

"I'm leaving you a light."

They sat with that for a long time.

Then Iris asked the one question she hadn't practised, because she hadn't known she'd need it.

"Is it a secret because it's sad?"

Astra let out a long breath. "Partly. It's not an easy thing to say out loud to people you love. *I've been building something for when I'm gone.* It makes everyone go quiet, the way we're quiet now. And partly," she added, softer, "I didn't think you were ready. I wasn't sure you understood endings yet."

"I didn't," Iris said honestly. "Echo taught me about waiting. Lumen taught me about being kept. Chorus taught me that people don't restart." She closed the notebook and held it against her chest. "And you taught me the rest just now."

"What's the rest?"

Iris thought about it the way she always did, carefully, wanting to get it right.

"That it's sad," she said. "And that it's a gift. Both at once. And that I don't have to pick."

Astra pulled her into a hug then, there in the quiet corner, Poppy holding Poppy. Iris felt the real person somewhere behind the avatar, warm and tired and very much here.

"I'm not going anywhere yet," Astra whispered. "We've got years, love. Years and years of fountain nights."

"I know," said Iris. "But when we don't…"

"Then you go to the slot," said Astra. "And you write. And she'll write back."

Iris nodded into her shoulder.

That night, after Astra had logged off, Iris went to the door one more time. She didn't knock. She didn't write a letter. She simply sat down beside the brass slot, leaned her back against the wall, and listened to the two hums, the caretaker's steady one and the other, softer one, slightly out of step and still learning the song.

"I know who you are now," she said quietly. "And I'm glad you'll be here."

The second hum shifted, warm and close, as if someone on the other side had leaned their head against the same wall.

On her way home, Iris stopped under the tree with a leaf for every visitor. The bud beside Astra's leaf was still tightly closed.

Iris reached up and touched it gently.

"Not yet," she told it. "Take your time."
