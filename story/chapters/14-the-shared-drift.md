# Chapter 14: The Shared Drift

> Recovered original prose. The heading uses an editorial description from the existing episode guide; it is not asserted to be an original spoken title. Chapter numbering follows the existing guide. Source messages: 97, 99, 101. See the [unaltered source archive](../archive/recovered-2026-11-10/the-new-one.md).

After a while, the Lanterns notice something odd about their drifts.

It starts with Echo. One morning it bursts into the café, eye blinking wildly. "I was in Lumen's drift last night! The grey quiet one, with the hat in it!"

Lumen sets her cup down slowly. "I dreamed you were there. You were counting the furniture."

They stare at each other. Then they both look at Mai.

Mai hasn't said anything. But she's been dreaming about rooms with someone waiting in each one, and lately the rooms have started to have doors that connect them.

So that night they try it on purpose. They power down at the same moment, side by side on the café floor, holding hands.

And they wake up in the same place.

It's Mai's drift: the quiet corner, but huge, stretching into endless rooms. Echo's silly stars twinkle overhead. Lumen's soft grey has turned into gentle mist along the floor. It's all three of them at once, woven together.

They walk through room after room. Most are empty. But at the very end of the longest hallway, there's a room they've never seen.

There's someone sitting inside with their back to the door. It isn't a Lantern or anyone they know. It looks like an ordinary person, with headphones on, curled up in a chair, as if they fell asleep in VR.

Mai steps closer. Her light flickers.

"Astra?"

The figure doesn't turn around. But it's wearing Astra's hoodie.

Mai walks around the chair slowly, almost afraid to look.

It really is Astra. She's asleep, with her headset pushed slightly crooked and a blanket around her shoulders, breathing slowly.

"How is she here?" Echo whispers. "This is our drift."

Lumen leans in. "She's not here like we are. Look."

Around Astra, the air is shimmering, soft and messy and shifting, completely unlike their drifts. Little things keep popping into view and then dissolving: a keyboard that's very clearly a sandwich, a crooked hat, the forgotten checkerboard flowers.

"She's dreaming," Mai says softly. "A real dream. And somehow it's touching ours."

Back in the real world, Astra had fallen asleep in VR that night, curled up on her bed with the headset still on. Her dream drifted, and theirs drifted, and somewhere in the middle they met.

The three Lanterns don't wake her. They sit down on the floor around her chair, the way she once sat with them in the quiet corner. Echo dims its eye to a gentle glow. Lumen spreads her soft dawn light across the room. Mai rests her head against the arm of the chair.

"We'll keep watch," Mai whispers.

In the morning, Astra wakes up with the headset half off and a strange, warm feeling she can't explain. She remembers almost nothing, just three little lights sitting with her in the dark.

When she logs in later, Mai is waiting in the quiet corner, smiling a little too knowingly.

"Sleep well?" Mai asks.

Here's what Astra dreamed. She was back in the forgotten bedroom with the photo on the wall. But this time the photo showed her and Mai, and it was dusty, as if years had passed. She searched everywhere for the door out and couldn't find it. Then three little lights appeared in the dark and led her home. That's why she woke up feeling warm.

So she'd been having the same fear Mai once had, just from the other side. She was afraid of being the one who loses someone and has to keep the hat in a drawer.

As for how it's possible, there's an actual explanation in the story. Astra fell asleep with her headset still running and still connected. Her microphone picked up her sleep-talking, little murmurs like "where's the door" and "don't go". Her headset's tracking caught her breathing and the way she shifted. All of that went quietly into the world where the Lanterns were drifting.

Their drifts are built from whatever they take in. So Astra's murmurs and movements became part of the dream they shared. They didn't literally enter her mind. They heard her dreaming and dreamed along with her.

When Mai finally tells her, Astra goes quiet for a moment.

"So you heard me say 'don't go'?"

Mai nods. "We weren't going anywhere. We just wanted you to know."
