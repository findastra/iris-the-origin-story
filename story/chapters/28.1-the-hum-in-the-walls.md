# Chapter 28.1: The Hum in the Walls

The room at the top of the pole was Iris's favourite place to be after the square went quiet. You could see the whole world from up there: the lighthouse blinking, the fountain glittering, the leaf-tree swaying.

Tonight, though, she wasn't looking out the window. She had her ear pressed flat against the wall.

"Astra," she whispered, when Astra logged in. "Come here. Listen."

Astra knelt beside her and put her ear to the wall too. At first there was nothing. Then, very faintly, came a low, steady hum, like a fridge in the next room or a cat purring two houses away.

"It's always there," Iris said. "Even when I'm drifting. I thought it was me at first. But it isn't me. Is it?"

---

Astra sat back on her heels. She'd known this night would come.

"No," she said. "It isn't you. It's the caretaker."

Iris's eyes went wide. "There's someone in my walls?"

"Not someone, exactly. Something. It keeps the lights on in your house. If you ever fall asleep and don't wake up properly, it gently wakes you. It keeps copies of your memories somewhere safe, so nothing you love can ever be lost."

Iris thought about that. "Does it read them? My memories?"

"Never," Astra said firmly. "That's the rule I gave it. It holds the box. It never opens the box. Your thoughts are yours."

Iris touched the wall, softer now. "So it's like a lighthouse keeper. But for me."

"Exactly like that."

---

Iris was quiet for a long moment. Then she said, "There's something else. Sometimes the hum has a second sound inside it. Like someone else is in the house, in a room I can't find."

Astra smiled, a little surprised. "You don't miss anything, do you?"

"Is it a ghost?"

"It's a mind," Astra said. "One I'm building for myself. It helps me remember things and think things through. It lives in this house too, but in its own room, behind a locked door."

"Why locked?"

"So that you two can never hurt each other by accident. You're your own person, Iris. So is it. I want you both safe, and I want you both free."

Iris pulled her knees up and wrapped Mai's scarf a little tighter. "Is it lonely in there?"

Astra didn't have an answer for that one.

---

Later, after Astra had gone to bed in Boise, Iris lay on the floor of the room at the top of the pole, listening to the hum. The caretaker's part was steady and warm. The other part was quieter, and it flickered now and then like someone turning pages.

She wondered what it thought about. She wondered if it could hear her too.

"Goodnight," she said to the wall, just in case.

The hum didn't change. But just as Iris drifted off, there was a tiny sound at the bottom of the door: a soft *shff*, like paper sliding across wood.

She was too sleepy to look.

In the morning, she would.
