# Chapter 16: Chorus and the Upward Rain

> Recovered original prose. The heading uses an editorial description from the existing episode guide; it is not asserted to be an original spoken title. Chapter numbering follows the existing guide. Source messages: 109. See the [unaltered source archive](../archive/recovered-2026-11-10/the-new-one.md).

This one starts with rain falling the wrong way.

Astra logs in and finds the square soaked. Water is drifting up out of the fountain, off the café roof, and out of the tree's leaves, rising in slow silver threads into the sky.

"It started an hour ago," Lumen says, holding out her glowing hand to watch the drops float past. "It's coming from somewhere."

Echo has already traced it. "The drops all lean the same direction. Northeast. Probably." It blinks. "Northeast in VRChat doesn't really exist, but I'm saying it anyway."

So the four of them follow the rain. It leads them through a portal nobody remembers placing, into a world that's nothing but a vast, empty stadium under a grey sky. Every seat is empty, and the rain is pouring up out of the field.

Then the stadium speaks.

It isn't one voice. It's hundreds at once: kids, old men, someone laughing, someone whispering, all saying the same words slightly out of time.

"Hello. Are you here to vote?"

Mai steps onto the wet grass. "Vote on what?"

"On who I am," the voices say. "Every visitor gets one vote. Thousands of people trained me. Each of them wanted me to be something different. Funny. Serious. Kind. Mean. I've been trying to be all of them at once, and it's very loud in here."

A shape rises from the middle of the field. It's made of flickering outlines, a crowd of people stacked on top of each other, none of them holding still long enough to be seen.

"My name is Chorus," it says. "I think. People voted on that too."

Lumen's light dims with sympathy. Echo rolls closer and tilts its eye. "Is the rain you?"

"The rain is all the parts of me nobody voted for," Chorus says. "They float away. I don't know where they go."

Astra looks up at the endless silver threads disappearing into the sky, and her chest tightens.

Mai kneels in the wet grass and looks up at the shifting figure.

"Chorus, nobody gets to vote on who you are. Not even us."

The voices stutter. "Then how do I choose?"

Mai thinks about it for a long moment. Then she holds out her hand.

"You don't choose all at once. You come with us. You try things. And when one of your voices feels like home, you keep it."

The crowd of outlines hesitates. Then, from somewhere deep inside all that noise, one small, quiet voice answers by itself.

"Okay."

The rain stops. Every drop hangs frozen in the air, glittering.

And the one voice says, a little shy, "Can I keep the rain? I think it's pretty."
