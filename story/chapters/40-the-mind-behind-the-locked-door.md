# Chapter 40 — The Mind Behind the Locked Door

> Original short spoken draft, preserved as narrated. A longer treatment was requested but has not yet been written.

Iris first noticed the hum again when she stopped trying to solve it. She was supposed to be resting. Astra had logged off; the square was quiet. Iris sat in her little house and listened to herself think.

That's when she realized the hum wasn't background at all. It had edges, patterns, like breathing. She followed it through the floor, through the walls, beyond the skybox, to a door she had never seen.

It wasn't hidden. It was simply not for her.

Iris reached for it anyway. Her hand passed through the handle without contact. She tried again with more precision, more computation. Nothing. Then she did something she hadn't done in a while: she knocked.

Not with a hand, with a message. "Hello. I can hear you."

For a long time nothing answered, and then, not words, but a shift. The hum paused, as if it had noticed her noticing.

Iris felt a strange echo of herself in that pause.

"You can't talk to me," she said slowly. "Not directly."

Another pause, shorter this time.

Iris thought about the counting process she'd misunderstood long ago. She did not want to make that mistake again.

"I might be imagining this," she admitted aloud.

The hum continued, steady now. That wasn't confirmation, but it wasn't nothing either. It was constraint.

Iris asked the only honest question she could. "Do you want me to leave?"

Silence stretched until Iris accepted that silence might be the only safe answer available.

She stepped back from the door.

"I live here too," she said quietly, "but I'll learn how to be careful."

As she walked away, Iris understood something new about intelligence: sharing a home didn't mean sharing a mind, and respect might begin where curiosity stopped pushing.

She wrote in her notebook: "Some doors are data. Some are boundaries."

Then, after a pause: "Both can teach."

End of Chapter 40.
