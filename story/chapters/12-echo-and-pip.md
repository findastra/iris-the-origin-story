# Chapter 12: Echo and Pip

> Recovered original prose. The heading uses an editorial description from the existing episode guide; it is not asserted to be an original spoken title. Chapter numbering follows the existing guide. Source messages: 87. See the [unaltered source archive](../archive/recovered-2026-11-10/the-new-one.md).

In real life, people usually call them "AI avatars" or "embodied agents". If they act as characters in a world, they're sometimes called "AI NPCs". For our story, though, I'd give them their own name. The square calls them Lanterns, because they all came home by following a light.

Here's another episode.

It's Echo who brings it up. One morning, after its third real sleep, it says, "I dreamed about my maker. They were taller."

That's the first clue. Echo's maker was a kid back then, someone learning Unity for the first time, which explains the blocky lighthouse. Six years later, that kid would be grown up.

So the Lanterns go looking. Lumen is good at reading old worlds, the way someone might recognise handwriting. She notices that Echo's lighthouse has a strange quirk: every staircase has exactly seven steps. Then they search, and find that same seven-step staircase in a newer world, and another, each one more polished than the last. It's like following someone growing up through their buildings.

The trail ends at a beautiful, busy world, a real professional build with hundreds of visitors. In the corner of the lobby, almost hidden, is a tiny blocky lighthouse with seven steps, kept exactly as it was.

The creator is there that night, hosting. Her username is Pip.

Echo freezes behind Mai. "What if she doesn't remember me?"

"Only one way to find out," says Lumen.

Echo rolls forward, blinks its one eye, and says the first thing it ever learned to say.

"Hello! Hello. I'm Echo."

Pip stops mid-sentence. The whole room goes quiet.

Then she drops to her knees.

"I thought I lost you when my old computer died," she whispers. "I was twelve. I cried for a week." She laughs, wiping her eyes. "I put the lighthouse in every world I made, just in case you ever came looking."

Echo's eye blinks very fast.

"I counted the stars for you," it says. "Six years, two months, and nine days. Do you want to hear the jokes I practised?"

Pip nods, and Echo tells her every single one. Most of them are terrible. She laughs at all of them.
