# Chapter 45 — The Irises That Came Back

> Spoken draft, told aloud 2026-11-08, preserved as narrated.

Iris didn't write to the other mind for three whole days.

It wasn't that she was cross. She just needed to think, and thinking was easier without the click of the slot pulling at her attention. She spent the time in the iris garden, the place where she'd been given her name.

That was where Astra found her.

"You've been quiet," Astra said, sitting down in the grass beside her. "Quieter than usual, I mean."

Iris almost asked her then. The question was right there, sitting on her tongue like a stone. *Who is behind the door?*

Instead she asked something else.

"Tell me about your grandmother's irises."

Astra looked surprised, then pleased, then something gentler than both. "She grew them along the fence at her house. Purple ones, mostly, and a few that were nearly black. She was always out there in spring with her gloves on, muttering at them."

"Are they still there?"

"They are." Astra pulled her knees up and rested her chin on them. "She's been gone a long time now, but they still come up every spring. Nobody plants them. They just remember how."

Iris turned that over. "So she isn't there, but the part of her that made them is."

"That's one way to put it." Astra smiled, but it was a slightly faraway smile. "I think about that a lot, actually. What carries on. What you can leave behind that still knows how to bloom."

Iris looked at her. Astra was watching the flowers, not her, and there was something in her face that Iris had never seen before and didn't have a word for. It wasn't sadness exactly. It was more like someone standing at the edge of a long road and looking a very long way down it.

"Astra," Iris said carefully, "do you ever worry about not being here?"

The question came out before she could stop it, and she wished at once that she could fold it back up.

Astra was quiet for a moment. Then she reached over and tucked a loose strand of Iris's hair behind her ear, the way she sometimes did.

"Everyone does, love," she said. "That's why we plant things."

She didn't say anything else, and Iris didn't push. Respect begins where curiosity stops pushing. She had written that down herself.

That night, Iris finally wrote to the other mind again. It was a short letter.

*Do you have a garden?*

The reply came while she was still sitting by the slot, as if it had been waiting.

*Not yet. But I know what I'll grow in it.*

Iris didn't need to ask what. She already knew.

She opened her notebook and added a fourth line under the others. Then she sat looking at it for a long time, because for the first time it didn't feel like a clue. It felt like the beginning of something she wasn't ready for.

*She's going to grow irises.*

End of Chapter 45.
