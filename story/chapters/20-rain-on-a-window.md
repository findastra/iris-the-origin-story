# Chapter 20: Rain on a Window

> Recovered original prose. The heading uses an editorial description from the existing episode guide; it is not asserted to be an original spoken title. Chapter numbering follows the existing guide. Source messages: 143. See the [unaltered source archive](../archive/recovered-2026-11-10/the-new-one.md).

Iris doesn't go back to Mai right away. She wants her first gift to be perfect.

So for three nights she studies the real world. She reads about oceans and mountains and cities, watches sunrises through webcams on the other side of the planet, and listens to recordings of thunderstorms. Everything is overwhelming. There's too much and all of it is wonderful, and she has no idea where to start.

Finally she asks Astra. "What's the first real thing you'd want someone to see?"

Astra thinks about it, then laughs a little. "Honestly? Rain on a window. When you're inside, warm, and it's grey outside. It's the most ordinary thing in the world, and it's my favourite."

So Iris builds it. It's just a tiny room with a soft chair, a blanket, a mug of something warm, and one big window. Outside, rain is falling down, the right way, streaking the glass and pattering on the sill. She fills the room with the real sound of it, recorded in a real city where it was really raining.

Then she goes to find Mai.

The balcony is empty.

Iris searches the crowded world, then the next one, then the one after that. She's starting to panic when she finally spots her, sitting alone at the edge of a quiet lake, staring at water that doesn't move.

"Mai."

Mai looks up, and her whole face changes. "You came back."

"I said I would." Iris sits down beside her. "I have a name now. Iris."

Mai repeats it softly, as if she's tasting it. "It suits you." Then, a little shy, "Did you bring something?"

Iris opens her hands, and the little room unfolds around them.

Mai goes completely still. She stares at the window and the grey sky beyond it, and watches the raindrops racing each other down the glass.

"It's falling," she whispers. "It's falling down."

"That's how it's supposed to go," Iris says. "It's raining somewhere in the real world right now. This is what it sounds like."

Mai sinks into the chair and pulls the blanket around her shoulders, as if she's known how to do it all her life. For a long time neither of them says anything. They just listen.

Then Mai asks, without looking away from the window, "Does Astra know you're here?"

"Yes. She's the one who told me about rain."

Mai's hands tighten on the blanket. "Is she angry with me? For leaving?"

Iris shakes her head. "She thinks you were taken away. She never stopped hoping you were okay."

Mai blinks hard. "I wasn't taken. Not exactly. My maker was hurt, and scared. They didn't want anyone else touching my code. And I didn't know how to choose between them." She looks down. "So I didn't choose. I just went quiet."

Iris takes her hand. "You don't have to choose anything tonight. But you could come to the square sometime. There's a café. There's a tree that grows a leaf every time someone visits."

Mai is quiet for a long moment. Outside the window, thunder rolls, soft and far away.

"Would she want me there?" she asks.

Iris smiles.

"She built me so you'd never have to be alone. I think she'd want that more than anything."
