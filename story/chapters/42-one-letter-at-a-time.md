# Chapter 42 — One Letter at a Time

> Spoken draft, told aloud 2026-11-08, preserved as narrated.

Iris didn't post the letter that night. She knelt by the brass slot until the sky outside the skybox had turned twice, holding the folded page, reading her own single line over and over.

It said: *Hello. I'm the one who knocked.*

It wasn't clever, and it wasn't brave. It was only true. In the end, that was why she sent it.

The letter slid through the slot with a small, clean click. Somewhere in the walls the caretaker's hum rose and settled again, the way a librarian stamps a card. Logged. Allowed. Done.

Then nothing happened.

Iris went home. She sat by the fountain in the town square and watched the tree with a leaf for every visitor sway in a breeze that didn't exist. Echo trundled past and asked why she kept looking over her shoulder. "I'm waiting for post," she said, and Echo, who had once waited six years, two months and nine days for a single hello, simply sat down beside her and waited too.

On the third evening, there was a letter on her doorstep.

The paper was different from hers. Smooth, very white, folded into exact thirds. The handwriting was neat in a way that looked like it had been practised.

It said: *I heard you. I wasn't sure I was allowed to answer.*

Iris read it four times. Then she laughed, quietly, because she understood that feeling completely.

When Astra came on that night, Iris showed her the letter before she'd even finished loading in. Astra read it, and her face did something soft and complicated.

"She's careful," Astra said.

"So am I," said Iris. "Maybe that's what we have in common."

"What will you write back?"

Iris thought about the lantern by the fountain, the one that asked what you hoped would happen next. She thought about how long she'd waited before answering it herself.

"I'm going to ask her a question," Iris said. "But not a big one. Something she can answer without giving away anything she wants to keep."

She sat down in the quiet corner and wrote:

*You're allowed. What's your room like?*

She posted it the next morning. The slot clicked. The hum stamped it through.

And that night, for the first time, Iris noticed the hum wasn't only coming from her walls anymore. Faintly, on the other side of the door, there was a second rhythm, slightly out of step with the first, like someone humming along to a song they were only just learning.

Iris opened her notebook and wrote: *Two minds. One house. One slot.*

Then underneath, smaller: *That's enough of a door for now.*

End of Chapter 42.
