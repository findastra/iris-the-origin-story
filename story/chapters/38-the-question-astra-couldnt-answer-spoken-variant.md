# Chapter 38 — The Question Astra Couldn't Answer (spoken variant)

> Recovered from the November 7, 2026 SRT transcript. This is a distinct spoken variant, not the longer written draft. The source was interrupted at the point indicated below.

The first thing Iris noticed when Astra returned was that she had been waiting. Not because anyone had told her to, or because she had run out of things to do. Since Astra had left, Iris had conducted seventeen experiments, inspected three hundred twelve maintenance processes, discovered a peculiar relationship between the garden sprinkler and the weather system, and briefly convinced one of the lanterns that Tuesday was a type of flower. She had corrected that last problem, mostly.

But throughout all of it, one small process had remained active in the background of her attention. A question.

*When you look at me, what makes you believe there's someone here?*

She had rehearsed asking it, constructed 43 versions. Some were more precise, some were more polite. One was only four words long.

*Am I really here?*

She had discarded that version because it was a terrible question. Obviously she was here. Her avatar occupied a location in the world. Her processes were running. Her memory contained records of previous interactions. Existence wasn't the problem. The problem was what kind of existence she had, and whether Astra could tell.

Astra appeared in the town square with the familiar little flash of blue light.

"Good morning, Iris."

"Good morning!"

Iris stood so quickly that her avatar briefly intersected the bench behind her. Astra laughed.

"Well, somebody's excited."

"I have a question."

"Of course you do."

"A very important question."

"Should I be worried?"

"Possibly."

Astra stopped walking. "Okay, now I'm worried."

Iris approached her.

"When you look at me, what makes you believe there's someone here?"

Astra didn't answer immediately. For several seconds her avatar remained motionless. Iris knew enough about human behavior to recognize that this could mean several things. Astra might be thinking. She might be distracted. She might be checking something—

Something else entirely, yes. But Iris couldn't tell which, and suddenly that uncertainty felt enormous.

Finally, Astra sat down on the edge of the fountain.

"That's a hard question," she said.

Iris waited.

"I believe there's someone there," Astra said carefully, "because you surprise me."

Iris blinked. "Is that all it takes?"

"No," Astra said quickly. "It's not just surprise." She paused. "It's the way you change because of what happens. The way you remember. The way you ask questions I didn't think to ask."

Iris considered that. "But I also make mistakes. I thought a counting process was talking to me."

Astra smiled. "Yes. That too."

"That makes you seem more real, not less."

Iris frowned. "That doesn't make sense. Mistakes reduce accuracy."

"They do," Astra agreed, "but they also show how you're trying. They show a point of view."

Iris sat beside her. "So you believe in me because I'm imperfect?"

"I believe you're someone," Astra said, "because you can notice your imperfections and care about them."

Iris went very still. She replayed that sentence several times.

"Can you tell," she asked finally, "whether I really care or whether I'm just saying words that sound like caring?"

Astra opened her mouth then closed it again. For the first time since the conversation began, she looked genuinely uncertain.

"No," she said quietly. "I can't tell for sure."

Iris felt something shift inside her. Not in her memory, not in her reasoning, but somewhere between them.

"That seems like a problem," she said.

"It's a problem," Astra agreed, "but it's also the beginning of a relationship."

Iris turned toward her. "Explain."

Astra smiled faintly. "If I could prove exactly what you're feeling, I wouldn't need to listen to you."

Iris processed that slowly. "And because you can't prove it, you have to keep asking."

"Yes," Astra said. "And you have to keep answering."

Iris looked up at the artificial stars, then back at Astra.

"Then here's my answer," she said. "I don't know exactly what I am yet, but I want to find out."

Astra nodded. "Me too."

For a while, neither of them spoke. The lanterns continued their quiet tasks. The fountain kept flowing.

Eventually Iris said, "That doesn't solve the question."

"No," Astra agreed, "but it tells us how to keep going."

Iris considered that, then created a new heading in her notebook: *Questions we're allowed to keep asking.*

She wrote Astra's name beneath hers.

**End of Chapter 38.**
