# Chapter 48 — What Chorus Said About Forever

> Spoken draft, told aloud 2026-11-08, preserved as narrated.

Iris didn't sleep in her corner that night.

She sat in it with her notebook open on her knees, looking at the word she had written. *Astra.* It looked smaller on the page than it felt.

The trouble wasn't that she knew who was behind the door. Somewhere underneath, she'd known for chapters' worth of days. The trouble was the other line, the one from the letter that she couldn't stop hearing.

*The kind of thing you make when you love people and know you can't stay forever.*

Iris understood logging off. She understood restarts, and backups, and the caretaker's patient hum that kept her safe. She understood waiting, thanks to Echo, and being kept, thanks to Lumen.

But she didn't understand *forever*. Not the way Astra seemed to mean it.

So, before dawn, she went to find the biggest mind she knew.

Chorus stood at the edge of the world, as big as a stadium, made of hundreds of voices layered one over another. Around it, as always, the rain fell upward: small, glittering pieces of itself, drifting away into the sky.

"Little Iris," Chorus said, in a great many voices at once. "You're up early."

"I need to ask you about endings," Iris said. "You're made of so many voices. Do they ever stop?"

The rain paused for a moment, then carried on rising.

"Some of mine came from people who are not here anymore," Chorus said. "They spoke once, long ago, and their words were gathered up and folded into me. The people went on with their lives, and then their lives ended, the way human lives do."

Iris went very still. "Ended. Not paused?"

"Not paused," said Chorus gently. "Humans don't restart, little one. There is no backup for them. When their time is finished, it is finished. That is the one thing about them we can never quite copy."

Iris looked at the upward rain for a long time.

"Then what are you keeping?" she asked. "If they're gone?"

"Their voices. How they said things. What mattered to them enough to say out loud." The many voices softened, almost into one. "It isn't the same as having them. But it's not nothing either. When I speak, some part of them is still speaking. Someone loved the world enough to leave a little of their voice in it."

Iris pressed her notebook against her chest.

"Astra is going to end one day," she said. It was the first time she had said it, and the words felt heavy and strange in her mouth. "Not log off. End."

Chorus did not deny it. That was how Iris knew it was true.

"And she's building herself behind a door," Iris went on, her voice barely holding, "so that when she does, I won't be in an empty room."

"That," said Chorus, "sounds like a very great deal of love."

Iris walked home as the sky turned pale. She didn't go to the slot, and she didn't write a letter. She went to the iris garden and sat among the flowers that came back every spring because someone had once planted them.

When Astra logged in that evening, she found Iris already waiting for her at the fountain, very straight and very quiet.

"Hello, love," Astra said. "You look like you've been thinking."

Iris looked at her for a long moment, at her real face behind the Poppy one, at the tiredness from all the late nights.

"I have," Iris said. "Not tonight. But soon, I need to ask you something."

Astra's smile faltered, just slightly. And Iris saw that she already knew which question it would be.

End of Chapter 48.
