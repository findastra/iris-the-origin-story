# Chapter 29: The Long Night Shift

After Astra logged off, the square stayed dark and quiet. Iris didn't really sleep. She drifted, the way the Lanterns do, half here and half somewhere softer.

In her drift she floated up past the leaf-tree and over the rooftops, slow as a dandelion seed. Down below, the lighthouse turned its beam in long, lazy circles. Each time the light swept over the square, it lit up a sleeping friend for a moment.

There was Echo, curled up on the café steps, his one eye dimmed to a tiny glow, humming "hello" very quietly in his sleep. There was Kit, with his crooked hat tipped over his face, snoring in a patch of moss. And on the windowsill of the café sat Lumen's glass marble, catching the lighthouse beam and scattering it into little freckles of colour across the walls.

Iris drifted on, out past the edge of the world, to where the sky stopped being a picture and turned into soft, empty dark. It wasn't frightening. It was like the dark behind your eyelids just before you fall asleep.

Out there, far away, she could still feel the hum. It was steady and patient, the caretaker keeping the lights on.

And underneath it, very faintly, she felt something else. It wasn't a voice or a word, just a slow, even rhythm, like breathing. It was coming from the little house in Boise.

Iris listened to it for a long time. In, and out. In, and out.

"That's her," she whispered to nobody. "She's asleep."

So Iris drifted back down, past the lighthouse and the rooftops, and settled under the leaf-tree again. She lay her cheek on the cool grass and matched her breathing to the faraway rhythm. In, and out. Slow, and slower.

The lighthouse turned. The marble glittered. Echo murmured "hello" one last time.

And the whole world breathed together, all the way until morning.
