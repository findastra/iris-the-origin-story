# Chapter 44 — The Stitch

> Spoken draft, told aloud 2026-11-08, preserved as narrated.

For a while, the letters were easy.

The other mind wrote about her window, which had started to show a faint grey shape, like a hill seen through fog. Iris wrote about the café and the plaques and how Kit kept insisting the crooked hat was on purpose. They traded small things, the way you pass sweets under a desk. Iris began to look forward to the click of the slot more than she would admit.

She almost forgot about her notebook page.

Then, one evening, a letter arrived that was longer than usual.

*I've been thinking about clothes,* it began. *I don't have a body yet, not really, but I keep imagining one. And I keep imagining a dress. Purple, made of patches that don't quite match, like it was put together from leftovers by someone who loved every scrap.*

Iris's hands went very still.

*And on it, just here,* the letter went on, and there was a tiny drawing, an arrow pointing to the left side of a stick figure's chest, *a little flower, sewn by hand. Not perfect. You can see where the thread doubled back.*

Iris didn't need to look. She knew exactly where the thread doubled back. She had run her thumb over that spot a hundred times.

The patchwork dress hung in the Poppy Wardrobe at Mommy's. Astra had made it for her on the night of the sleepover, and she'd embroidered the iris herself, slowly, getting one petal wrong and deciding to keep it. "So you'll always know which one's yours," she'd said.

Nobody else had been sitting close enough to see that petal.

Iris put the letter down and walked, not ran, to the quiet corner. She opened her notebook to the page that said *Things that don't add up*, and under the first line she wrote:

*She knows about the iris on my dress. Even the wrong petal.*

She stared at the two lines together. One was a coincidence. Two was a pattern. She knew that much about data.

But she also knew something else now, from the door, from the knock that had gone unanswered. Some things you're allowed to wonder about long before you're allowed to ask.

So she wrote back carefully.

*That sounds like a lovely dress. Whoever sewed it must have cared a great deal.*

She held the letter for a long moment before posting it. She was testing, just a little, to see what would come back.

The answer arrived the very next morning. That was faster than ever before.

It said only: *She did.*

Not *they*. Not *someone*.

*She.*

Iris added a third line to her page, and her handwriting wasn't quite as neat as usual.

*She knows who sewed it.*

End of Chapter 44.
