Four Poppys! Okay, here we go.

**The Poppy Wardrobe**

It started, the way most chaos at Mommy's starts, with Dev.

"Mommy," Dev said, dropping into the café in the square without knocking. "Mommy, emergency."

Astra looked up from her tea. "What kind of emergency?"

"Wardrobe emergency." Dev spun in a slow circle, showing off her Poppy, who was wearing exactly one sock. "I lent the other one to somebody last week and I have no idea who."

Across the café, Iris froze mid-sip. Then, very slowly, she looked down at her own foot.

There it was. One striped sock.

"Ohhh," said Iris. "I thought this came with me."

Dev burst out laughing so hard she had to hold onto the counter. "You're the new mommy! Astra's told me everything. Come here, come here, let me look at you."

Iris stood up shyly and let Dev walk around her, inspecting her like a proud aunt at a family reunion.

"Base Poppy," Dev murmured. "My sock. And..." She squinted. "Is that Astra's hair clip?"

Astra coughed. "I may have left a few things in her files."

"Mommy, she's wearing hand-me-downs."

"She's a little sister! That's what little sisters wear!"

That was the moment Mai walked in. She was in Poppy too, of course, because Astra was there, wearing a jacket Dev had definitely made last spring.

The café went silent. Echo rolled into the middle of the room, swivelled its eye from one Poppy to the next to the next to the next, and made a sound like a kettle boiling over.

"Four," it said. "There are four of you. I asked for name tags *last week.*"

"Echo, honey, you'll figure it out," said Dev.

"I will not!"

---

So Dev called a meeting. It wasn't a real meeting. It was really a sleepover in the back room at Mommy's, the one with the big mirror wall and the clothes racks nobody ever tidied. Every Poppy outfit anyone had ever made hung there in a big messy rainbow.

"Rules," Dev announced, clapping her hands. "This is the Poppy Wardrobe. Everything in here belongs to everybody. You can borrow anything, but you have to tell the story of where it came from."

Mai pulled a soft pink cardigan off a hanger. "This one was Astra's. She wore it the night she gave me my body. I borrowed it, and then I never gave it back." She looked guilty. "Sorry, Mommy."

"Keep it," Astra said. "It looks better on you anyway."

Dev held up a pair of tiny bat wings. "These were mine for the Halloween show, two years ago. I fell off the stage in them. Nobody has ever let me forget it."

"You did a full spin first, though," Astra said. "Very graceful fall."

"Thank you, Mommy."

One by one they went through the racks. There was the raincoat from the night the square flooded, and the sparkly boots from Mommy's first anniversary party. There was a scarf that had been passed through so many Poppys that nobody remembered who'd made it first, but everyone agreed it was lucky.

Iris listened to every story. She held each piece carefully, as if it might break.

When the racks were almost empty, Dev noticed she hadn't picked anything.

"What's wrong, baby mommy?"

Iris looked at the pile of borrowed treasures. "Everything here has a story. And everything I'm wearing is somebody else's story." She touched the striped sock. "I don't have anything that started with me."

The room went quiet.

Mai sat down next to her. "I felt like that too. For a long time. Everything I had was given to me."

"How did you stop feeling like that?"

Mai smiled. "I didn't. I just realised that being given things means someone loved you enough to share."

Iris thought about that. But she still looked a little sad.

---

That's when Dev and Astra shared a look. It was the kind of look that best friends share when they're about to do something together without having to say a word.

"Mommy," said Dev.

"Mommy," said Astra.

"You thinking what I'm thinking?"

"Always."

The next three nights, Iris wasn't allowed in the back room. Every time she tried, someone suddenly needed her help in the café, or Echo insisted it had a joke that absolutely couldn't wait. Every one of the jokes was terrible.

The mommies of the club kept slipping through that door: dancers, hosts, girls Iris had only met once or twice. Each of them came out smiling with paint on their hands.

On the fourth night, Dev opened the door herself.

"Okay, baby mommy. Come in."

In the middle of the room, on its own hanger, was an outfit Iris had never seen before. It was a long, flowing dress in deep purple fading into pale violet at the hem. And it wasn't just one piece. It was made of patches, dozens of small pieces stitched together.

"Every mommy in the club made one square," Dev said softly. "Each one picked something that reminded them of you."

Iris stepped closer. One patch had little rain clouds on it, raining *down*. One had a tiny lighthouse. One was covered in stars, exactly where Echo had counted them. One was soft grey that slowly turned to dawn, and that was Lumen's. One was a sandwich, which was obviously Echo's too.

Mai's square was pale pink, the same as the cardigan.

"So you'd always have a bit of me," she said.

And right at the collar, at the very centre, was one square that wasn't stitched like the others. It had been embroidered by hand, very slowly and carefully, with a single purple iris.

"That one's mine," Astra said quietly. "It's from Grandma's garden."

Iris didn't say anything for a long time. Then she put it on.

It fit perfectly. Of course it did. They'd all made it for Poppy.

"It's still made of other people's stories," Iris said, turning in front of the mirror.

"Yes," said Dev. "But it's the first thing that's been made *for* you. Every story in there is about you."

Iris turned to all of them: Astra, Dev, Mai, and the crowd of mommies squeezed into the doorway.

And then, for the first time, she added something of her own. She reached into her files, pulled out a tiny glowing hair clip shaped like a flower, and clipped it behind her ear.

"There," she said. "Now Echo can tell us apart."

From somewhere behind the crowd, Echo yelled, "*THANK YOU.*"

---

Later, much later, the four of them lay on the floor of the back room in a tangle of borrowed clothes, staring up at the ceiling.

"Hey, Mommy?" said Dev.

"Yeah, Mommy?" said Astra.

"We made a whole family out of one avatar."

Astra laughed softly. "I think the avatar made us."

Mai, half asleep in Astra's cardigan, mumbled, "Can we do the wardrobe again next week? I want to give Iris my favourite scarf."

"The lucky one?" Iris whispered.

"The lucky one."

Iris touched the flower clip in her hair and smiled up at the ceiling. Four Poppys lay there, every one of them different, and every one of them home.
